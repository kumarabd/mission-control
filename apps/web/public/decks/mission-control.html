Example: Todoist pings you at 9pm but doesn't know you've skipped three days. A weekly Claude schedule like "check my spending every Monday" starts from words each run and re-explains the whole thing. Muse can book the dinner and keep working after you close the app.

"You can do this today. Reminder, notes and tracker apps are dependable, but they only do what you set up, piece by piece. AI agents with schedules, like Claude Code's, understand you, but a schedule is a prompt on a timer: every run starts from words, not from a structure."

"Meta's Muse closes that gap: a personal agent that keeps working after you close the app. So capability is no longer the difference. The difference is whose computer it runs on, which is the next slide."

Be fair to both: apps are great at what they do, and agents are great at reasoning. The gap is the combination.

1:40 · Whose computer (35s)

Example: Ask the room: "Where does your spending history live?" With Muse, on Meta's VM, along with the logins for every account you connect. With Mission Control, in a database in your own workspace, and you pick the model, even one running on your laptop.

"Meta's Muse is the closest thing to what we built: a personal agent that keeps working after you close the app, connects to your apps, remembers you. Be generous about that."

"The difference is where it lives. Muse runs on a dedicated virtual machine in Meta's cloud: that VM holds the agent, your data and the logins for every service you connect, and the model is Meta's. Mission Control is the opposite shape: you own the model choice, the connectors, the data and the engine that runs it. Even the model can run on your own hardware."

Be accurate, if asked: Meta says each VM is isolated, shows an audit trail, lets you export files, disconnect services and opt out of training, and has announced a Confidential VM with a key only the user holds, not yet available. Meta says the data is not shared with its advertising system. TIME (Oct 2026) reported that Muse builds continuously updated profiles of users and the people they mention. Meta did not dispute it. Those are their claims and TIME's reading of internal instructions, not our testing.

If asked about choice or self-hosting: Meta's own developer docs (dev.meta.ai) confirm it publishes open-weight models, Muse Spark Open and Muse Glimmer (Apache 2.0), to run on your own GPUs; they say nothing about the consumer Muse agent, which runs only on Meta's cloud, and I found no source saying you can swap its model. Custom connectors: a third-party test (Parallel, mid-Sept 2026, citing Meta's Help Center, not independently confirmed) says Muse builds its own client on its cloud VM for a remote server you point it at. It is reached from Meta's cloud, so a server on your laptop is not reachable, and Meta does not review custom connectors. Pricing reports conflict ($16 or $20 and $80 or $100 a month), so leave it off the slide.

2:15 · How (40s)

Example: You skip your walk three days running. The engine notices and tells the agent. The agent doesn't scold: it proposes "walk 10 minutes after lunch". Tomorrow's reminder fires on time either way, with no model involved.

"Here's how we get both. The agent does the thinking: it understands the sentence, asks what it needs, and designs the structure. It hands that to a deterministic engine that stores the goal, the steps, the routine, the reminder, the view, and does the numbers. The engine runs the clock: it fires on time, every time, no model needed."

"And when you skip days, the engine notices and tells the agent, which adapts: a smaller step, a different nudge. The agent brings judgement; the engine brings guarantees."
